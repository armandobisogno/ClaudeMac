## «De me scripti sunt». The Two Writings of Humanities Research and the Legitimacy of Artificial Intelligence
## Abstract
The debate on the legitimacy of generative AI in humanities research is usually framed in terms of detection and control, as if academic writing were a single object. This paper argues that every research text in the humanities combines two different writings. The first, documentary writing, reconstructs, summarises, collates and compares sources, producing what the author has elsewhere called the «augmented source-object». The second, which the paper calls «sentimental» in Schiller’s sense, is the writing in which scholars reflect on the impression their sources make on them and account for their own encounter with the past. Drawing on Augustine’s distinction between the books of the Confessiones written «de me» and those devoted to Scripture (Retract. II, 6, 1), on Eugenio Garin’s «two missions» of the historian of philosophy, and on the proposal of a «hermenethics» of humanistic knowledge, the paper takes a position: delegating documentary writing to large language models is legitimate, and even desirable, provided it remains verifiable, declared and checked; sentimental writing is not forbidden to the machine but cannot be delegated, because it rests on a guarantee no system can provide – someone who answers for it. An experiment in which an AI agent wrote a paper on Augustine from the author’s own corpus is discussed as a test case. Agency in digital writing is distributed; responsibility is not.
## Keywords
Artificial intelligence; Academic writing; Hermeneutics; History of philosophy
