@title Hermenethics: The Responsibility of Interpretation in AI-Assisted Humanities Research

## Abstract

The use of large language models in textual interpretation is usually judged by output accuracy. This article argues that the decisive normative question lies elsewhere, in the responsibility of the one who judges. Drawing on Augustine’s *De magistro*, where signs do not teach but only prompt the learner to seek, it proposes to read text generated by artificial intelligence (AI) as admonition rather than instruction: material that can open an inquiry but cannot close it. On this basis the article develops “hermenethics” (hermeneutics plus ethics), a framework in which interpreting a source means answering for it, to someone and before someone. The framework is tested on a distinction Augustine already draws: some writing may be dictated to a scribe, some may not (*nec ista dictari debent*, *Soliloquia* 1.1.1). The case is an experiment in which, using AI tools, a paper on Augustine’s autobiographical writing was generated entirely from the Latin corpus, theoretically ready for publication. Such a technical product can legitimately be delegated, since its value lies in the result and the scholar retains the competence to verify and rewrite it. Yet it lacks three relations that make a text one’s own: a reflection it was cut from, a later answerability (no one can write its *Retractationes*), an addressee. Writing through which one progresses (*scribendo proficere*, *Epistula* 143.2) cannot be delegated without ceasing to be what it is. Human oversight is thus redefined as a non-delegable interpretive responsibility, with consequences for research documentation and for teaching critical AI literacy.

## Keywords

Hermenethics; delegation; AI-assisted writing; Augustine; epistemic responsibility; medieval philosophy

## Planned structure of the article

- 1. From accuracy to responsibility: the normative turn in AI-assisted humanities research.
- 2. *Signa admonent*: Augustine’s *De magistro* (esp. 10.33–11.38 and 14.45) and the place of judgement.
- 3. Hermenethics: interpretation as answerability; the source as an “augmented object” and the situated interpreter (Bisogno 2025).
- 4. *Nec ista dictari debent*: delegable and non-delegable writing in Augustine (*Soliloquia* 1.1.1; *Epistula* 143.2; *Retractationes*, prologue).
- 5. The case: an AI-generated paper on Augustine’s autobiographical writing and what it lacks (a prior reflection, a later answerability, an addressee); competence as the condition of legitimate delegation.
- 6. Consequences: transparency about the machine’s role, verification against critical editions and attribution of interpretive choices as documentation practices; peer review and the teaching of critical AI literacy.
